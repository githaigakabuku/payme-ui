PM-00 Access · Authentication

Sign In Page

payme.app / login
What's on this screen

A centered login card on a deep violet gradient backdrop, carrying the PayMe wordmark and its tagline, "Contract & Payment Management." Underneath, a simple username and password form leads with a full-width Sign in button, and the footer quietly reinforces the promise: secure contract management.

Every subscription business runs on trust before it runs on revenue. This is the front door to that trust — built on JWT authentication, role-based access control, and audit logging, so the businesses using PayMe can hand out access with confidence instead of duct tape.

PM-01 Overview · Command Center

Dashboard Page

payme.app / dashboard
What's on this screen

The moment an admin signs in, they land on four live counters — Clients, Contracts, Invoices, Templates — followed by a Quick Actions rail (add a client, create a contract, create an invoice, manage tiers) and a Recent Activity feed tracking subscription tiers, invoices, and audit entries.

This is the platform's answer to a founder's hardest question — "is the business actually working?" Instead of stitching together spreadsheets and bank statements, they get one screen that monitors performance and points straight at the next action, turning revenue visibility into something you glance at rather than calculate.

PM-02 Customers · Relationship Records

Clients Page

payme.app / clients
What's on this screen

A clean directory table listing every customer by Client, Company, Email, Phone, and Status, with an Add Client action sitting top-right and a running total of clients on record.

Behind every recurring invoice is a real relationship, and PayMe treats the client record as the source of truth that contracts, invoices, and tiers all point back to — the foundation of customer lifecycle management for agencies, educators, consultants, and healthcare providers alike.

PM-02.1 Form

Add New Client Form

modal · new-client

A focused modal captures everything a contract or invoice will later need: Name, Email, Company, Contact Person, Phone, Tax ID, and a full Address field, then commits it with a single Create Client action.

Onboarding a client here takes seconds, not a spreadsheet template — exactly the kind of common billing groundwork PayMe exists to absorb so founders can spend that time on their product instead.

PM-03
PM-03 Agreements · Legal Trail

Contracts Page

payme.app / contracts
What's on this screen

Every agreement lives here with its Contract name and description, a Status badge (e.g. Draft), its Created date, and inline actions to Version, Sign, or open Details — plus a one-click Create Contract shortcut.

Contracts are where a business relationship becomes enforceable, and versioning plus e-signing here means the paper trail is never lost between a founder's inbox and a client's desk — a secure audit trail baked into the workflow itself.

PM-03.1 Form

Create New Contract Form

modal · new-contract

Pick a Client, optionally start from a saved Template, then set a Title, Description, and the full Content of the agreement before creating it.

Letting a template pre-fill this form is the shortcut that turns a one-off legal document into a repeatable business process — write it once, reuse it for every client on that tier.

PM-04
PM-04 Plans & Pricing · Monetization

Subscription Tiers Page

payme.app / tiers
What's on this screen

A pricing table listing each plan's Name, Display Name, Monthly and Yearly price, usage Limits, Features, and Status — empty until an Add Tier defines the first plan.

This is the flexible subscription-plan engine at the heart of PayMe's pitch: instead of hard-coding "Basic / Pro / Enterprise" into a codebase, any business can design and adjust its own recurring-revenue ladder from a UI.

PM-04.1 Form

Create New Tier Form

modal · new-tier (1/2)
modal · new-tier (2/2)

The top half sets Basic Information — a system Name and a customer-facing Display Name — plus Monthly and Yearly Pricing, with a built-in hint to price the annual plan at roughly a 20% discount. Scrolling down, Usage Limits cap Max Clients, Max Contracts, and Max Templates (or unlimited via -1), and a Features list is built one tag at a time.

Pricing, limits, and features in one form is what makes the platform genuinely modular and easily customizable — any business can define exactly what "Basic" versus "Enterprise" means for them, without a single line of code.

PM-05
PM-05 Billing · Cash Flow

Invoices Page

payme.app / invoices
What's on this screen

A running ledger of billing activity: Client, Amount, Status (e.g. draft), and Due Date for every invoice, with a Create Invoice button ready for the next one.

This is where recurring revenue becomes real, collectible cash — professional invoices generated in seconds, with statuses that make payment reconciliation something the system tracks instead of something a founder chases over email.

PM-05.1 Form

Create New Invoice Form

modal · new-invoice

Select the Client, give the invoice a Title, set the Amount and a Due Date, then generate it — no separate billing tool required.

Paired with the planned Stripe integration and secure webhook processing, this small form is the seed of a full payments pipeline — from "invoice created" to "payment received" in one connected system.

PM-06
PM-06 Reusable Documents · Standardization

Contract Templates Page

payme.app / templates
What's on this screen

A library table of reusable agreements, organized by Name, Description, and the Tier they're linked to, with an Add Template action to grow the library.

Templates are the shortcut between "Contracts" and "Tiers" — write the standard agreement for a pricing plan once, and every new client on that plan starts from a polished, consistent document instead of a blank page.

PM-06.1 Form

Create New Template Form

modal · new-template

Set a Name, optionally link it to a Tier, add a short Description, then draft the full template Content that future contracts will inherit.

This closes the loop across the whole platform — clients, tiers, contracts, and templates all reference one another, so the business logic of "who gets billed what, on what terms" stays consistent everywhere it appears.
